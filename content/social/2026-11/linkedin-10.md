## Post (paste-ready)

Behavioral health SEO dies a little every time a clinician leaves.

The autopsy finding I see most on group practice sites:

A clinician moves on. Someone deletes their page. That URL spent two years earning searches for their name and their specialty. Now every one of those searches lands on a 404.

The fix takes ten minutes. Pull them off every concern, location and telehealth page the same day. Then redirect their old URL to the specialty page that fits their work. The searches keep landing on your practice instead of an error.

The searches are there. In the 90 days ending Oct 4, 2026, our own Google Search Console showed our site 223 times for "behavioral health seo" at an average position of 75, with no page built for it. When Google digs that deep, page one is thin.

That's why technical SEO is part of every month we run a group practice site. A small behavioral health practice needs someone watching the 404 report and setting redirects, because Google cannot rank a page that no longer exists.
