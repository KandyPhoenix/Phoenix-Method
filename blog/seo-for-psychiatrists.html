Why Psychiatry Search Is Not Therapy Search

Most mental health SEO advice is written for therapists. Psychiatry searches look different, and your site has to match how people actually search.

  • The searcher often already has a referral. A primary care doctor or therapist said “you should see a psychiatrist.” Now they are checking whether you are real, whether you take their insurance, and whether you are accepting new patients.
  • They search by service, not modality. “Medication management,” “psychiatric evaluation,” “ADHD evaluation for adults,” “psychiatric nurse practitioner near me.” Each is a different page.
  • Telehealth changes the map. A telepsychiatry practice can see patients anywhere its clinicians are licensed. If the site never says which states, Google cannot match you to anyone.
  • The stakes are higher. Psychiatry content falls under Google’s “Your Money or Your Life” standard. Credentials, authorship, and accuracy carry more weight than on almost any other kind of site.

The Autopsy: Where Psychiatric Practice Websites Die

Nothing hides, nothing survives unexamined. When we open up a small psychiatry practice site, these are the usual causes of death:

  • One “Services” page doing the work of six. Medication management, evaluations, ADHD, telepsychiatry, and anything specialized all crammed into one list. Google cannot rank one page for six different searches.
  • Provider bios with no credentials. A headshot and a paragraph, but no MD, DO, or PMHNP, no board certification, no training, no states of licensure. For a YMYL site, that is a trust gap Google notices.
  • A new clinician who is not on the site. You hired a psychiatric nurse practitioner four months ago. The website still lists two providers. Patients searching for an NP will never find them.
  • An insurance list that is wrong. You dropped a panel last year. It is still on the site. Every wrong listing is a wasted intake call.
  • An “accepting new patients” notice from years ago. Or a waitlist notice nobody took down.
  • No telehealth page. Telepsychiatry is mentioned in a footer line, with no states, no process, and nothing for Google to index.

Psychology Today is not your website. A directory profile is useful. It also sends every visitor to a page full of other psychiatrists. Your own site is the only place where you control the information, the intake path, and the authority you build. Use the directory. Do not let it be the only thing Google can find.

The Battle Plan: The Pages a Psychiatric Practice Needs

Rankings follow structure. A small psychiatry practice does not need a hundred pages. It needs the right ones, each built for one search:

  1. One page per core service. Medication management. Psychiatric evaluation. ADHD evaluation and treatment. Any specialized service you genuinely offer. Each page says who it is for, what the first visit looks like, and how to book.
  2. A full bio for every prescriber. Degree, board certification, training, licensure states, and the populations they work with. Add Person schema with credentials so Google can connect the clinician to the practice.
  3. An insurance and fees page. Every panel you are actually on, private-pay rates if you publish them, and what superbills look like for out-of-network patients. Keep it current.
  4. A telepsychiatry page. Which states, which services are available by video, and what a patient needs to get started.
  5. Practice schema. MedicalClinic or Physician markup with your address, hours, and services, so Google knows exactly what kind of healthcare entity you are.

Local SEO and Google Business Profile for Psychiatrists

For an in-person practice, the map pack is where the patient decides. Your Google Business Profile needs the right primary category (“Psychiatrist” is a real category, use it), accurate hours, the correct address or service area, and a link to the matching page on your site, not just the homepage.

Keep your practice name, address, and phone number identical across the profile, the website, and the directories. Inconsistent listings make Google less confident about which one is right.

Reviews need extra care in psychiatry. Never reply in a way that confirms someone is or was a patient, even to thank them. A short, general response that invites the person to call the office protects you and still shows the profile is active. We cover the full framework on our local SEO page.

The Real Problem: Nobody Is Running the Website

Here is the part most SEO advice skips. The fixes above are not hard. They are just constant.

A new clinician joins. A panel changes. You add telehealth in a new state. Your hours shift for the summer. Every one of those is a website update, and in most small practices there is no one whose job it is to make it. The office manager is drowning in prior authorizations. The agency that built the site stopped answering. So the site slowly drifts out of date, and rankings drift with it.

That is what dead rankings look like: not one disaster, but a hundred small updates that never happened. The practices that climb are the ones where somebody owns the site every month: content updates, technical checks, the GBP profile, and a steady stream of pages built around what patients search. It is the kind of patient work that outranks agencies charging three times as much.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SEO for psychiatrists different from SEO for therapists?

Yes. Psychiatry searches are usually service-based (medication management, psychiatric evaluation, ADHD evaluation) and often come after a referral, so searchers are checking insurance, availability, and prescriber credentials. The site needs separate service pages, detailed prescriber bios, and an accurate insurance page rather than modality pages alone.

Do I still need a website if I have a Psychology Today profile?

Yes. A directory profile helps people find you, but it sits next to other psychiatrists and you do not control the page. Your own website is where you build authority, keep insurance and availability current, and send patients straight to your intake process.

How long does SEO take for a psychiatric practice?

It depends on your market, your site’s starting condition, and how consistently it is maintained. Technical fixes and accurate service pages can be indexed within weeks, while ranking movement for competitive searches typically takes several months of steady work. Anyone who promises a specific ranking by a specific date is guessing.
