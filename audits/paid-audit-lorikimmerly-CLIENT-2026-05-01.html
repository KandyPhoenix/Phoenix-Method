Executive Summary

Lori Kimmerly Therapy is a licensed therapy practice based in Renton, Washington, offering individual, couples, and family therapy both in-person and via telehealth across Washington state and Arizona. The practice employs a diverse team of more than a dozen licensed and associate-level therapists, with specializations spanning Internal Family Systems (IFS), anxiety, depression, trauma, couples counseling, and culturally responsive care.

For YMYL sites, E-E-A-T signals are not optional enhancements; they are ranking prerequisites. The most impactful work ahead is not small fixes — it is building the content infrastructure that will establish this practice as the definitive IFS and therapy authority in the Washington market.

The robots.txt file is correctly configured. No therapy pages, bio pages, or content pages are accidentally blocked from search crawlers.

Structured Data (Schema)

✓ MedicalBusiness Schema on Homepage

The homepage correctly identifies the practice as a medical business — the appropriate schema type for a therapy practice. This gives Google a verified signal about the nature and category of the site.

✓ Person Schema on Therapist Bios

Most therapist bio pages include Person schema, which signals to Google that these are real licensed practitioners. This is a meaningful E-E-A-T signal and supports potential rich results for therapist name searches.

⚠ Schema Requires Ongoing Monitoring — WordPress Can Override It

Structured data (schema) is one of the most valuable SEO signals on the site — but it requires active maintenance. WordPress, the Avada theme, and the Fusion Builder page editor can silently overwrite schema during routine page saves and content updates. This is a known behavior across WordPress sites using visual page builders: a template save or content edit can strip structured data that was previously in place, with no warning.

We monitor for this as part of ongoing monthly maintenance. When schema is overwritten — which happens periodically — we identify it and re-implement it. This is not a one-time setup; it is an active, recurring part of what we do to keep the site's structured data signals intact and working correctly for Google.

Crawlability

⚠ Author Archive Pages Are Indexed — No SEO Value

Two internal author archive pages are currently indexed in Google. These pages contain thin content, have no meta descriptions, and inadvertently expose the names of behind-the-scenes admin accounts rather than the therapists themselves — a potential concern for a YMYL site where Google evaluates who is creating and managing health content. Removing them from Google's index eliminates the thin-content issue and the admin-exposure concern.

⚠ Blog Category Pages Have Duplicate Titles and Thin Content

The blog category pages currently share the same title as the blog index page and contain minimal unique content. As the blog grows, we will give each category page its own optimized title and introductory content, transforming these from duplicate pages into useful entry points for specific topic searches.

⚠ No Terms of Service or Therapy Disclaimer
