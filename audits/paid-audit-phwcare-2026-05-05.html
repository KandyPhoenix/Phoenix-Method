Executive Summary

Parish Health and Wellness (PHW) is a licensed mental health counseling practice based in Red Bluff, California, offering both in-person therapy and telehealth services to clients across the entire state. The practice provides a broad range of evidence-based services — including treatment for anxiety, depression, trauma, substance addiction, family conflict, and LGBTQ+ affirming care — and accepts Medi-Cal, Medicare, and most major commercial insurance plans. PHW's commitment to serving underinsured and underserved communities in Northern California sets it apart from most competitors in the region.

This audit covers every publicly indexed page on phwcare.com — 32 pages crawled across the site's full depth — analyzing technical infrastructure, on-page optimization, schema markup, content strategy, E-E-A-T signals, and local SEO posture. The site has meaningful strengths: HTTPS is enforced with correct redirect behavior, schema markup is present on every page, service pages have substantial content depth (1,800–3,300 words each), and a bilingual privacy policy demonstrates commitment to Spanish-speaking clients. However, the audit reveals a recurring pattern of technical formatting errors that are actively undermining the site's credibility with search engines — most critically, a sitewide title tag overflow problem that affects 14 of 25 core pages, a near-total absence of image alt text across all service pages, and multiple critical on-page structure errors.

As a mental health and substance abuse practice, phwcare.com is classified by Google as a YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) site — subject to the most rigorous quality standards under Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines. E-E-A-T signals (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) are not optional enhancements for YMYL health sites — they are ranking prerequisites. PHW currently lacks individual therapist bio pages with verifiable credentials, author attribution on blog posts, and the kind of structured trust content that Google quality evaluators actively look for. These gaps are addressable and their resolution will have direct, measurable impact on rankings for PHW's highest-value keywords.

Technical SEO Audit

D Grade: D — Critical Formatting Errors Sitewide

HTTPS & Redirects

✓ HTTPS Enforced (301 Redirect)

HTTP traffic correctly redirects to HTTPS via a 301 permanent redirect. This is confirmed working and is a strong baseline signal for Google.

✓ www Canonical Is Consistent

The site uses www.phwcare.com as the canonical domain. The homepage self-references correctly. This is consistent throughout all crawled pages.

XML Sitemap

✓ Sitemap Present at /sitemap.xml

A valid XML sitemap was found at /sitemap.xml and includes blog posts, blog categories, and other content pages with lastmod dates and priority values. This is a correct Squarespace-generated sitemap.

✗ Sitemap Not Listed in robots.txt

While the sitemap exists, it is not referenced in robots.txt via a Sitemap: directive. This is a missed signal — search engine crawlers that process robots.txt first will not automatically discover the sitemap file location. Google will still find it if submitted via Search Console, but the robots.txt omission removes a passive discovery path.

Fix: Add the following line to the end of robots.txt (via Squarespace Settings → Advanced → External API Keys → or inject via code): Sitemap: https://www.phwcare.com/sitemap.xml — Note: Squarespace generates robots.txt automatically, so this may require adding a custom header rule or contacting Squarespace support.

⚠ Key Service Pages Not Confirmed in Sitemap

The crawled sitemap includes blog posts and category pages but service pages under /therapy/* are not confirmed in the sitemap XML. If Squarespace is not automatically including these pages, they may be discovered by crawl but not submitted directly — missing the freshness signal that lastmod dates provide.

Fix: In Google Search Console → Sitemaps → submit https://www.phwcare.com/sitemap.xml. Review the "Indexed" vs "Submitted" counts. If service pages are missing, verify each is marked "Public" in Squarespace page settings (not Password Protected or Draft).

Robots.txt

✓ robots.txt Valid — Core Pages Not Blocked

robots.txt is present and blocks appropriate paths: /config, /search, /account, /api, /static. No therapy service pages or content pages are accidentally blocked. Standard Squarespace-generated robots.txt is in place.

⚠ Cart Page Disallow Missing

The /cart page is not blocked in robots.txt and is actively indexed. Cart pages have no therapeutic content, no H1, no meta description, and word counts under 300 — a thin, low-quality indexed page that signals poor site quality to search crawlers.

Fix: Squarespace's native robots.txt cannot be easily edited. The best fix is to add a noindex meta tag to the cart page via Squarespace's page-level SEO settings: Settings → Page SEO → check "Hide from search engines." This will prevent indexing without modifying robots.txt.

Title Tag Length — Sitewide Crisis

✗ 14 Pages with Title Tags Exceeding 60-Character Limit

This is the most widespread technical issue on the site. Squarespace automatically appends " — PHW Counseling & Online Therapy" (34 characters) to page titles where the SEO title field was not explicitly set — or where it was set to include the brand name, causing double-appending. Google truncates titles beyond approximately 600px (roughly 55–60 characters) in search results, replacing overflow with "..." This directly degrades click-through rates for 14 of 25 core pages. The worst offenders:

  • /contact: 96 chars ("Contact PHW Counseling | Schedule Online Therapy in California — PHW Counseling & Online Therapy")
  • /privacy-policy-es: 96 chars
  • /about: 92 chars
  • /mental-health-resources-california: 94 chars
  • /privacy-practices: 95 chars
  • /insurances: 85 chars
  • /therapy-downtown-red-bluff: 89 chars
  • /counseling-jobs-red-bluff: 89 chars
  • /therapy-red-bluff-ca: 75 chars
  • /online-therapy-california: 84 chars
  • /phw-counselors: 73 chars
  • /mental-health-blog: 63 chars
  • /rehab-center-donations: 60 chars (borderline)
  • Homepage: 65 chars
Fix: For each affected page, open Squarespace Page Settings → SEO → Title field. Set a clean SEO title that is UNDER 26 characters — Squarespace will append " — PHW Counseling & Online Therapy" (34 chars) making your total under 60. Do NOT include brand name in the title field — it gets added automatically. See Priority Action Plan for full rewrite recommendations for all 14 pages.

Meta Description Length Violations

✗ 8 Pages with Meta Descriptions Exceeding 160 Characters

Meta descriptions beyond 160 characters are truncated by Google in search results, cutting off calls to action and key differentiators. The worst offender is the rehab donations page with a 714-character meta description (copied from page body text). Other over-length pages include /therapy/family (173 chars), /therapy/lgbtqplus-issues (170 chars), /therapy/conflict-resolution (180 chars), homepage (170 chars), /therapy/anger (168 chars), and /mental-health-resources-california (177 chars).

Fix: In Squarespace Page Settings → SEO → Description, set each to 150–158 characters maximum. Write action-oriented copy ending with a clear CTA (e.g., "Call 530-528-2342" or "Medi-Cal accepted"). Leave the rehab donations page meta blank or write a clean 150-char summary — never paste body text into this field.

H1 Tag Issues

✗ Blog Index Page Has 6 H1 Tags

The /mental-health-blog page has 6 separate H1 elements — one for the blog title and one for each featured post headline. Search engines expect exactly one H1 per page to identify the primary topic. Multiple H1s confuse crawlers about what the page is about.

Fix: In Squarespace blog settings, confirm that individual post titles are rendered as H2 or lower on the blog index page. The only H1 should be the blog section title. If blog post cards are using H1, this requires a Squarespace template or CSS override via Settings → Advanced → Code Injection: article .entry-title { display: block; } /* override H1 to visual-only */

✗ /online-therapy-california Has 2 H1 Tags

The online therapy landing page has two H1 elements: "Online Therapy in California" and "Benefits of Online Therapy." This is a priority landing page for a high-value keyword and a dual H1 directly undermines its authority signal.

Fix: In Squarespace page editor, locate the "Benefits of Online Therapy" heading and change it from H1 to H2. Only the primary page headline should be H1.

⚠ H1 Text Has Missing Spaces on 7 Service Pages

Multiple service page H1 elements appear to have missing spaces or merged words in the crawled HTML — for example: "Trauma FocusedTherapy," "Online Therapy California&In-Person Mental Health Services," "Onlinefamilytherapy near you," "Conflictresolutiontherapy & help near you." This may indicate a Squarespace Fluid Engine text block formatting issue where line breaks in the editor are not rendering as spaces. If these display correctly in a browser, they may only affect crawlers — but crawler-visible formatting IS what Google reads for ranking signals.

Fix: Audit each service page H1 in Squarespace editor. Ensure there are no line breaks between words where a space is intended. Use a single text block for each H1 with explicit spacing. Test by fetching the page URL via Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool → "View Tested Page" → check rendered HTML.

Schema Markup — Technical Implementation

⚠ LocalBusiness Schema Duplicated on Every Page

Every crawled page on the site contains two LocalBusiness schema objects — one appears to be site-wide code injection and another appears from Squarespace's built-in structured data. Duplicate schema of the same type can cause confusion for search parsers and may trigger Google Search Console validation warnings.

Fix: Review your Code Injection (Settings → Advanced → Code Injection → Header) for LocalBusiness or MedicalBusiness JSON-LD. If a manual LocalBusiness schema is injected there, check whether Squarespace is also generating one natively. Remove whichever is the duplicate — keep the more complete/accurate one.

✓ WebSite, Organization, and MedicalBusiness Schema Present on Homepage

Homepage includes WebSite, Organization, LocalBusiness, and MedicalBusiness schema types. This is a strong sitewide foundation. WebSite schema enables potential Sitelinks Searchbox eligibility.

✓ FAQPage Schema on /therapy-red-bluff-ca and /online-therapy-california

Two pages have FAQPage schema implemented. Note: Google deprecated FAQ rich results in May 2026, so this no longer generates SERP visual features. The schema still provides semantic value for AI Overviews. Consider whether to maintain or remove.

✗ No FAQPage Schema on Any of the 10 Condition Service Pages

Service pages for anxiety, depression, trauma, family therapy, and all other conditions have no FAQPage schema despite being the highest-value ranking pages. FAQ schema enables rich results — accordion-style expansions beneath the search listing — which dramatically increase click-through rate and SERP real estate. Every service page should have a minimum of 3–5 FAQ pairs with schema.

Fix: Add 3–5 FAQ pairs to each service page via Code Injection (page-level). Example for the anxiety page: Q: "Does online anxiety therapy really work?" A: "Yes — studies show CBT delivered via telehealth is as effective as in-person..." Add the FAQPage JSON-LD schema referencing the same questions. This is a high-priority fix that will produce visible SERP changes within 2–4 weeks of Google re-crawling.

✗ No Person Schema for Individual Therapists

The /phw-counselors page lists therapist names but has no Person schema identifying each clinician's credentials, license type, or specialty. Person schema for therapists directly supports E-E-A-T and is a prerequisite for rich results on practitioner searches (e.g., "Linda Spyres therapist Red Bluff").

Fix: When individual therapist bio pages are created (see Content Strategy section), add Person schema to each with: name, jobTitle, credential (LCSW, LMFT, etc.), url, and sameAs links to Psychology Today or other directory profiles.

Page Speed

Squarespace Fluid Engine sites generally score 55–70 on mobile due to optimized asset delivery — but content-heavy pages with many images (17–23 images on service pages) can be impacted by unoptimized images, especially given the 80–90% missing alt text rate indicating image blocks may not have proper lazy-load configuration.

Fix: Run manual test at pagespeed.web.dev for https://www.phwcare.com. Target: 70+ mobile, 85+ desktop. Key focus: image sizing (ensure no images are larger than display size), and review LCP — the largest contentful paint is likely an above-fold hero image. Squarespace handles CSS/JS bundling automatically, so image optimization is the primary lever available.

E-E-A-T Assessment (Critical for YMYL)

D Grade: D — Strong Privacy Compliance, Missing Core Trust Signals

Mental health counseling is one of Google's highest-scrutiny YMYL categories. Google's quality raters are instructed to assess whether practitioners are licensed, whether the site provides accurate medical information, and whether real credentialed humans stand behind the content. The following assessment evaluates all E-E-A-T signals found and missing on phwcare.com.

What's Working

✓ Privacy Policy in English and Spanish

PHW has both an English privacy policy (/privacy-policy) and a Spanish-language version (/privacy-policy-es) — a strong signal of commitment to the bilingual California communities the practice serves. The HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices (/privacy-practices) is also present. This is excellent for a healthcare YMYL site.

✓ Physical Address and Phone Number Present

22425 Sunbright Ave, Red Bluff, CA 96080 and (530) 528-2342 are confirmed in the site's LocalBusiness schema and visible on the contact page. NAP (Name, Address, Phone) consistency is a local trust signal.

✓ Team Page Exists

The /phw-counselors page lists therapist names and credentials (LCSW, LMFT, associate-level designations), establishing that real licensed professionals provide the services. This is a baseline E-E-A-T requirement for a therapy practice.

✓ Insurance Transparency

A dedicated /insurances page and prominent insurance information in meta descriptions demonstrate transparency about financial accessibility — an important trust signal for Medi-Cal and Medicare-accepting practices serving lower-income populations.

Critical E-E-A-T Gaps

✗ No Individual Therapist Bio Pages

This is the single most impactful E-E-A-T gap on the site. Google's quality raters evaluate therapy sites by asking: "Can I verify that a licensed, qualified professional provided this service?" A team overview page lists names but provides no verifiable depth. Competitors who publish individual bio pages with license numbers, educational backgrounds, specializations, and Psychology Today profile links demonstrate far stronger E-E-A-T — and rank accordingly.

Fix: Create individual bio pages for every therapist (minimum 400 words each): name, license type and state license number, education (university, degree), years of experience, specializations, treatment modalities, and a personal note about their approach to therapy. Add a headshot, sameAs links to Psychology Today and LinkedIn profiles, and Person schema with credential markup. URL format: /therapists/[first-last] or /therapist-bios/[name]. This is the highest E-E-A-T investment available.

✗ No Author Attribution on Blog Posts

Blog posts on /mental-health-blog are not attributed to named therapists. For a YMYL health blog, anonymous authorship is a significant E-E-A-T red flag. Google quality raters specifically check whether health content is written by qualified professionals or contains accurate medical information from identified sources. Anonymous blog posts can cause a YMYL site to receive a "low quality" content rating regardless of the accuracy of the content itself.

Fix: For every blog post, add an author byline linking to the therapist's bio page. In Squarespace blog post settings, set the author to a specific therapist. Add an "About the Author" section at the bottom of each post with credentials and license type. This also adds Article schema with the author property — a direct E-E-A-T schema signal.

✗ No Client Testimonials or Reviews Section on Site

Social proof is an E-E-A-T signal — particularly the "Trustworthiness" component. While PHW may have Google or Psychology Today reviews, there is no on-site testimonials section. Prospective clients who are not yet familiar with the practice need to see third-party validation before scheduling.

Fix: Add a testimonials section to the homepage and /about page. Include 4–6 verified patient quotes (obtain written consent per HIPAA guidance — testimonials must be voluntarily submitted by clients, not solicited about specific treatments). If direct testimonials raise HIPAA concerns, use anonymized first-name-only or initials format, or display a Google Reviews widget showing star ratings without quoting specific clients.

⚠ No Terms of Service Page

While the privacy policy is present, there is no Terms of Service or Terms of Use page. For a healthcare website with a contact form and potentially an online booking system, a Terms of Service establishes legal clarity about how the website and its information may be used — another trust signal for both users and crawlers.

Fix: Add a simple /terms-of-service page. Squarespace has a Terms of Service template. A basic page covering: service description, no guarantee of outcomes, emergency disclaimer (if you are in crisis call 988), and contact information for complaints — is sufficient.

Site Architecture & Internal Linking

Current Structure

PHW's site architecture is generally sound — a clear hub-and-spoke model with service pages organized under /therapy/[condition]. The navigation structure supports crawlability and the URL scheme is logical. However, several architectural issues exist that limit both user experience and link equity flow:

  • Orphaned utility pages: /therapy-folder/ and /therapylocations are crawlable alternate URLs that canonical to other pages, creating unnecessary crawl budget waste and potential confusion in Search Console coverage reports
  • /therapy hub page is thin: The /therapy page (356 words, no H2s) serves as the parent of all 10 service pages but provides insufficient content to rank for "therapy services California" — it should be expanded to a rich hub with intro paragraphs for each service, internal links to each service page, and FAQs about the practice overall
  • Blog categories lack meta descriptions: Category pages (/mental-health-blog/category/*) are indexed without meta descriptions, which reduces their SERP appeal and signals thin content to Google quality systems
  • No internal linking from service pages to each other: Each service page appears to be largely self-contained. Cross-linking between related service pages (e.g., linking from trauma page to EMDR page, from depression to life transitions) builds topical authority across the service cluster

Recommendations

  • Expand /therapy hub page to 800+ words with a 2–3 sentence description of each service and an internal link to that service page — this creates a proper content hub that supports the whole service architecture
  • Add "Related Services" internal links to the bottom of each service page — e.g., the anxiety page should link to depression and trauma pages; the substance addiction page should link to family therapy and life transitions
  • Noindex or remove /therapy-folder/ and /therapylocations — they serve no unique purpose and add crawl confusion
  • Add meta descriptions to all blog category pages — one sentence describing the category topic and a CTA ("Explore our therapist-authored articles on [topic]")
  • Add internal links from blog posts to service pages — each blog post should link to the most relevant service page using keyword-rich anchor text (e.g., "our anxiety therapy services" linking to /therapy/anxiety)
  • Create a site footer with links to all 10 service pages — if not already present, a footer services menu ensures every page on the site carries internal links to the key service pages, distributing link equity sitewide
