Why Dental Is Different

Local, Urgent, Insurance-Driven, and Brutally Competitive

Almost every dental search carries local intent. Patients rarely drive across town for a general dentist — they pick from whatever shows up in Google's 3-pack when they search. Emergency dental searches are even more urgent: pain is driving the click, and the patient is choosing within 30 seconds. Cosmetic and specialty searches (Invisalign, veneers, implants) carry higher-intent commercial queries where ranking even one page below the top results costs you real visibility on qualified patients.

On top of the competitive pressure, dental SEO has insurance complexity most SEO agencies ignore. "Dentist that takes Delta Dental," "dentist payment plans," "Medicaid dentist near me" — these are high-converting queries that generic agencies never bother to target. We do.

Frequently Asked

Dental SEO Questions

How is dental SEO different from general SEO?

Dental is one of the most competitive local SEO categories in the country. Patient search is heavily local and urgency-driven — "dentist near me," "emergency dental," "pediatric dentist [city]." Insurance, financing, and specialty positioning all shape content strategy. Generic SEO tactics underperform; dental-specific playbooks outperform.

How long until I see new patients from SEO?

Dental SEO timelines vary by local competition and starting state. Map pack and local levers tend to move first with aggressive GBP and citation work; organic rankings for high-intent queries like "general dentist [city]" or "emergency dentist" build over a longer horizon; specialty terms (Invisalign, cosmetic dentistry) are a longer game in most competitive markets. We prioritize the fastest-moving levers first.

Should I specialize my site or market everything equally?

Specialization ranks better and converts better. A general dentist who creates dedicated, well-optimized pages for cosmetic work, Invisalign, emergency services, and pediatric dentistry outperforms one whose homepage lists everything with equal emphasis. Dedicated pages let Google understand what you offer and rank you accordingly.

Do I need separate content for each insurance I accept?

Yes — or at least a dedicated insurance/financing page. Patients search "dentist that takes [insurance]" and "dentist payment plans." Capturing that search intent converts directly to new patients who might otherwise skip past you.

What about patient reviews — do those help rankings?

Hugely. Review velocity (how often you get new reviews) and quality (star rating, length, keyword usage) are direct local ranking signals. Dental patients are willing to leave reviews when asked — we install systematic review generation so it happens without manual effort.

Can you handle multi-location practices?

Yes. Multi-location dental SEO requires a location-page architecture, per-location GBP management, and careful duplicate-content handling. We've done it for multi-practice groups and know where the common mistakes are.
