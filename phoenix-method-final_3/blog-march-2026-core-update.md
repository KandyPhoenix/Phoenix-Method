# Google's March 2026 Core Update: What Changed and What to Do

**Published by Phoenix Method | March 26, 2026**
**Category: Algorithm Updates**

---

The March 2026 Core Update rolled out between March 10 and March 27 — and it hit hard. Over 55% of monitored websites saw ranking shifts within the first two weeks. SEMrush Sensor volatility scores spiked to 9.5 out of 10. Some businesses reported organic traffic drops of 20-35% overnight.

If you're in healthcare, dental, mental health, or any YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) niche, this update demands your attention. Here's what actually changed, who got hit, who gained, and exactly what you should be doing right now — without panic.

## What Made This Update Different

This wasn't a routine tweak. Two things made March 2026 unusual.

First, it landed just weeks after Google's first-ever Discover-only core update finished on February 27. Back-to-back algorithm changes within a month is rare, even by Google's standards. Many site owners got whiplash — their Discover traffic shifted in February, then their organic rankings reshuffled in March.

Second, industry analysts believe this is the first core update leveraging Google's Gemini 4.0 Semantic Filter, which means Google is now using its most advanced AI to evaluate content quality at a level we haven't seen before.

The March update rolled out over a 19-day window, with a notable second wave of ranking changes around day eight that caught many off guard.

## The Five Biggest Changes

### 1. Information Gain Is Now a Major Ranking Factor

Google is now measuring how much genuinely new information a page contributes compared to content that already ranks. This is a seismic shift.

Pages that reword existing top results without adding original data, case studies, or unique perspectives are losing ground fast. Sites that invest in original research and proprietary insights saw an average visibility gain of 22% during this update.

**What this means for you:** If your content can be found word-for-word (or close to it) on ten other websites, it's now a liability. Every page on your site needs to answer the question: *what does this page offer that nobody else's does?*

### 2. E-E-A-T Requirements Tightened Again — Especially for Healthcare

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness requirements got stricter, particularly for YMYL topics like health, finance, and legal content.

The data is stark: 72-73% of top-ranking pages now display detailed author credentials, up from about 58% before the update. Google is no longer giving the benefit of the doubt to anonymous or poorly attributed content in sensitive niches.

For healthcare and therapy practices, this means your therapist bios, clinical credentials, license numbers, and professional affiliations are now directly tied to your search visibility. A page about "anxiety therapy" written by "Staff" is losing to a page written by a named, licensed clinician with verifiable credentials.

**What this means for you:** Every piece of YMYL content on your site needs a named author with visible credentials. If you run a dental practice, your blog posts should be attributed to your licensed dentists. If you run a therapy practice, your service pages should reference your therapists' specific training and license numbers.

### 3. AI-Generated Content Is Under Real Scrutiny

The March update doesn't penalize AI-assisted content outright — Google confirmed at the 2025 Search Central Meetup that AI content "isn't inherently problematic." The violation happens when content is published primarily to generate SEO traffic at scale without meaningful human oversight.

The targeting is specific: pages that read fluently but add nothing that can't be found elsewhere. Content that combines AI efficiency with genuine human expertise remains unaffected. Mass-produced AI content without editorial oversight is now a ranking liability.

**What this means for you:** Using AI tools to help draft content is fine. Publishing 50 AI-generated blog posts a month without having a human expert review, edit, and add original perspective to each one is not. Quality over quantity has never been more literal.

### 4. Core Web Vitals Are Being Evaluated Site-Wide

Technical performance crossed a new threshold in this update. Google appears to be evaluating Core Web Vitals more holistically across your entire site rather than page by page. A fast homepage doesn't save you if your blog posts are slow.

The three metrics that matter:

- **Largest Contentful Paint (LCP):** How fast your largest on-page element loads. Target: under 2.5 seconds.
- **Interaction to Next Paint (INP):** How quickly your site responds to every user interaction — not just the first click, but every tap and scroll. 43% of sites still fail the 200ms threshold.
- **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS):** How much your page elements jump around while loading.

Data indicates 47% of sites with performance issues experienced ranking drops during this update. Google isn't punishing slow sites directly — it's promoting faster competitors above you.

**What this means for you:** Run every key page through PageSpeed Insights (pagespeed.web.dev), not just your homepage. If your site is on Squarespace, Wix, or a bloated WordPress theme, you may be fighting an uphill battle on speed. Image optimization, lazy loading, and minimizing third-party scripts are your quickest wins.

### 5. Local and Discover Signals Were Recalibrated

The February Discover update tightened which sites appear in Google Discover feeds — the number of unique domains in US Discover feeds dropped from 172 to 158. Google is showing users more locally relevant content, reducing clickbait, and favoring original reporting over rehashed material.

The March core update compounded this by adjusting how local relevance factors into organic rankings. Proximity data and behavioral signals now influence rankings more than before, particularly for service-based businesses.

**What this means for you:** Your Google Business Profile, local citations, NAP consistency, and geo-targeted content matter more than ever. If you serve a specific area, make sure your content reflects that geography naturally — not just in a footer address, but in your service pages, blog posts, and meta data.

## Who Won and Who Lost

**Winners:**
- Sites with original research, proprietary data, and expert commentary
- Industry-specific publications with demonstrated first-hand experience
- Healthcare sites with clear author credentials and clinical expertise
- Businesses with strong local SEO foundations and active Google Business Profiles
- Sites that invested in Core Web Vitals and technical performance

**Losers:**
- Sites with mass-produced AI content published without editorial oversight
- Coupon and deal sites with thin, dynamically generated pages
- Content aggregators that reword existing top results without adding value
- YMYL sites without clear authorship or demonstrated expertise
- Sites with inconsistent technical performance across pages
- Publishers relying on sensational headlines and clickbait for Discover traffic

## What to Do Right Now

Don't panic. Don't start rewriting everything on day one. Google themselves recommend waiting until the rollout is complete before drawing conclusions. The March 2026 rollout officially finished on March 27 — so now is the time to act.

### Step 1: Measure the Damage (or the Gains)

Open Google Analytics 4 and compare your organic traffic for the two weeks before March 10 against the two weeks after. Look at both sessions and engaged sessions — sometimes overall traffic holds but engagement drops, which signals you're ranking for different queries or attracting different visitors.

Open Google Search Console and check impression and click trends in the Performance report. Look at changes at both the query level and the page level. Core updates often affect individual pages rather than entire sites.

Check Discover traffic separately — in Search Console, navigate to Performance and select the Discover tab.

### Step 2: Audit Your E-E-A-T Signals

Review every service page and blog post on your site. Ask:

- Is there a named author with visible credentials?
- Can a visitor verify that author's expertise (license numbers, professional profiles, LinkedIn)?
- Does the content demonstrate first-hand experience, not just textbook knowledge?
- Are there trust signals visible on every page (privacy policy, contact information, professional affiliations)?

For healthcare and therapy sites: add therapist license numbers, link to professional directory profiles, mention specific training and certifications, and include real (anonymized) clinical scenarios that demonstrate hands-on experience.

### Step 3: Eliminate Thin and Duplicate Content

Identify any pages that don't add unique value. If you have blog posts that say the same thing as fifty other sites, either add original perspective and data or remove them. Thin content anywhere on your domain now drags down the quality assessment of your entire site.

Quality dispersion is real — strong content loses weight when it's surrounded by many weak pages on the same domain.

### Step 4: Fix Technical Performance Across Your Entire Site

Run PageSpeed Insights on your homepage, your top 5 service pages, and your most-visited blog posts. Fix anything below a 90 mobile score. Priority fixes:

- Compress and convert images to WebP format
- Enable lazy loading for below-the-fold images
- Minimize third-party scripts and trackers
- Fix layout shifts caused by ads, images, or dynamic content

### Step 5: Strengthen Your Local SEO Foundation

Update your Google Business Profile: complete every field, add recent photos, post weekly updates, and respond to every review. Audit your NAP consistency across all citations. Create or update geo-targeted content for the specific areas you serve.

### Step 6: Add Original Value to Existing Content

Don't just refresh dates and republish. Real freshness means new facts, new data, new examples, or a better structure that reflects current reality. Google confirmed that updating dates without updating substance is a weak move.

For every key page, ask: after reading this, does the visitor know something they couldn't learn anywhere else?

## The Bottom Line

The March 2026 Core Update is not a punishment — it's a recalibration. Google is getting better at distinguishing between content that genuinely helps people and content that exists primarily to capture search traffic. The businesses that treat their website as a resource for their customers rather than a keyword-targeting machine will continue to rise.

If you saw drops, there's a path back. It starts with honest assessment, not panic.
